Case Study

NVIDIA Nemotron Reasoning

A stratified Chain-of-Thought sampling method that cuts LoRA fine-tuning gradient variance by 83% on a 30-billion-parameter Nemotron model — measured, not estimated.

Co-authored with M Irfan Al Hakim (Universitas Negeri Semarang) · Preprint submitted to Elsevier · View source on GitHub ↗

01 — The Problem

Random sampling makes a 30B-parameter fine-tune fight itself

Fine-tuning NVIDIA's Nemotron-3-Nano-30B on Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning data under constrained GPU hardware isn't just an engineering inconvenience — it's an optimization problem. A typical CoT corpus pools wildly different reasoning domains together: numeral conversion, unit conversion, text encryption, bit manipulation, equation transformation. Sample from that pool at random, and the token distribution swings sharply from one mini-batch to the next. That swing destabilizes the gradient: loss spikes, gradient norms fluctuate, and VRAM allocation becomes unpredictable enough to trigger fatal out-of-memory crashes mid-run. On constrained hardware, losing a 30B fine-tune to an OOM crash partway through means losing hours of compute. The question we set out to answer: could restructuring how training data is sampled — not the model, not the optimizer — fix that?

02 — What I Did

Stratified the training data, then measured the difference instead of assuming it

Working with M Irfan Al Hakim, I built a stratified CoT sampling framework for LoRA fine-tuning:

  • Stratified data pipeline — partitioned a 2,907-example CoT corpus into 6 mutually exclusive reasoning strata (numeral conversion, gravitational-constant calculations, unit conversion, text encryption, bit manipulation, equation transformation), each roughly 16–17% of the data, and sampled proportionally from every stratum with a fixed seed (42) for reproducibility — instead of letting domain composition drift randomly batch to batch.
  • Hardware-constrained training engineering — LoRA (rank 32, targeting all linear layers) on Nemotron-3-Nano-30B, with dynamic CUDA allocator segment expansion to stop memory fragmentation, non-reentrant gradient checkpointing to trade recomputation for lower activation memory, and a compressed effective batch (micro-batch of 1, 4 accumulation steps) under AdamW with Cosine Annealing.
  • Step-level diagnostics — a custom training callback that logged gradient norm, cross-entropy loss, and VRAM allocation at every single training step, not just per epoch, so the comparison would be measured rather than eyeballed.

We then ran two configurations under identical hyperparameters — simple random sampling as the baseline, and stratified sampling as the proposed method — specifically to isolate the effect of the sampling strategy itself, not conflate it with anything else.

The headline isn't the lower loss — it's the 83% drop in gradient norm variance. That means the stratified run wasn't just a little better on average, it was dramatically more stable step to step, which is what actually prevents the fragmented memory spikes that cause OOM crashes on constrained hardware.

The honest caveat, stated plainly in the paper itself: this validates the effect on one model, one 2,907-example dataset, and one hardware setup. Testing this on larger models and full-parameter training regimes is explicitly future work, not something we've already shown. What we can say is that, under the conditions we actually tested, how you sample a heterogeneous CoT dataset measurably changes how stable a LoRA fine-tune is — independent of the model or optimizer.

Stack & Reasoning

A few of the calls, and their trade-offs

TechniqueWhyTrade-off
LoRA (rank 32, all-linear)Parameter-efficient fine-tuning that fits a 30B model into constrained VRAMLess expressive than full fine-tuning — a deliberate trade for feasibility
Stratified sampling (6 strata, seed 42)Balances reasoning-domain composition in every batch instead of letting it drift randomlyNeeds a corpus with identifiable domain labels — doesn't generalize to unlabeled data without extra work
Non-reentrant gradient checkpointingTrades recomputation for lower activation memory during backpropSlower per-step wall-clock time in exchange for fitting in VRAM at all
Effective batching (micro-batch 1 × 4 accumulation)Simulates a batch of 4 without the memory cost of holding it directlySmall true batch size per step, offset by accumulation rather than eliminated
